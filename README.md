## Overview

Valkyries (Old Norse: valkyrjur, singular valkyrja, meaning 'chooser of the slain') are a group of female divine beings who serve Odin, the Allfather, in Norse mythology. Their primary function is to determine the outcome of battles: they ride over battlefields, selecting which warriors will die and which will survive. The warriors chosen by the valkyries become the einherjar — the chosen slain — who are escorted to Odin's hall Valhalla (or alternatively to Freyja's field Fólkvangr) to prepare for Ragnarök, the apocalyptic battle at the end of the world. In Valhalla, valkyries serve as handmaidens to the einherjar, bringing mead and sustenance. The Old English cognate wælcyrge appears in several manuscripts. Valkyries are depicted as powerful, spear-wielding women in helmets and chainmail, mounted on horseback; they are associated with ravens and wolves (the battlefield scavengers sacred to Odin). They are also sometimes portrayed as 'swan-maidens' capable of shapeshifting by donning feathered cloaks. Archaeological finds include silver amulets from approximately 800 CE depicting women with ponytails, long dresses, swords, and shields — likely valkyrie figures. Scholars propose that valkyries evolved from 'demons of death' associated with battlefields into romantic figures capable of loving mortals. The Prose Edda lists 29 valkyrie names across various sources. Famous individual valkyries include Brynhildr, Sigrún, and Hildr. Valkyries connect in complex ways to other Norse female divine beings including the dísir (female spirits), norns (fate-weavers), and the goddess Freyja.

## Etymology

- **Origin**: Old Norse valkyrja, a compound of valr (the slain on a battlefield, literally 'corpses of the slain') and kjósa (to choose). The compound therefore means 'she who chooses the slain.' The Old English cognate wælcyrge derives from the same Germanic root.
- **Meaning**: Chooser of the slain
- **First use**: Old Norse literature of the 9th–13th centuries; the Old English form wælcyrge appears in Anglo-Saxon manuscripts from approximately the same period

## Physical Description

- **Morphology**: In the oldest sources, valkyries are primarily functional supernatural agents rather than fully characterized individuals — terrible battlefield spirits associated with death and conflict. In later saga literature, individual valkyries become romantic heroines (Brynhildr, Sigrún) with complex personalities and love stories. Archaeological silver amulets from approximately 800 CE depict valkyrie-type figures with characteristic attributes.

## Theories & Explanations

### Paranormal / Cryptozoological

**Literal divine battle-spirits** _(Historical Norse religious belief)_

Traditional Norse religious view: valkyries are genuine supernatural agents of Odin who literally appear on battlefields to choose the slain, reflecting the Norse warrior belief system in which battle death was divinely ordained and led to Valhalla.

### Scientific

**Evolution from battlefield spirits to romantic heroines** _(Old Norse scholars broadly, Jónas Kristjánsson, Hilda Ellis Davidson)_

Scholarly interpretation: valkyries evolved in the literary tradition from terrifying battlefield spirits associated with the violence of death (older layer) into romanticized female heroes capable of love and defiance (younger saga layer). This reflects broader shifts in Norse literary culture.
